— What is XPS?

X-ray Photoelectron
Spectroscopy

XPS is a technique used to read the chemistry of a material’s surface.

By exposing the surface to X-rays and measuring the energy of the emitted electrons, it identifies the elements present within the top 5–10 nanometres and reveals their chemical state.This makes XPS a powerful tool for understanding surface composition, oxidation states, and interfacial chemistry.

Reference Pages ↗
🔬

The Photoelectric Effect

When X-rays strike a surface atom, they can eject a core electron. Measuring the electron’s kinetic energy allows the binding energy to be calculated, which is the basis of XPS analysis.

📐

Surface Sensitivity

XPS probes only the top 5–10 nm of a material. This makes it ideal for studying surfaces, coatings, catalysts, and thin films.

📊

Chemical State & Quantification

XPS peak positions change with oxidation state and bonding environment. This allows identification of chemical species and estimation of their relative amounts.

⚗️

Applications

XPS is widely used in materials science, catalysis, corrosion, and energy research. In my work, it is a key tool for investigating Cu₂O nanostructures, linking surface chemistry and electronic structure to photocatalytic performance.
